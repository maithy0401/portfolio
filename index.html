How I work

From goal to release, and back again

This is the loop I follow for each LOTTE FLEX feature. Design is only one of the six steps.

What we measure becomes the next round of backlog items.

  1. Frame

    Start from the business goal and what users actually do.

  2. Specify

    PRD, user stories and acceptance criteria everyone can test against.

  3. Prioritise

    Order the backlog with Business and Development.

  4. Design

    End-to-end flows for customers and for the staff who support them.

  5. Verify

    Implementation review and UAT before each release.

  6. Measure

    Tracking plan, funnels and reports that show whether it worked.

AI in my workflow

I use AI for the routine first drafts, then review the output against the business rules and decide.

I expect AI to take on more of the routine work in product teams. People will still have to decide which problem is worth solving, and catch the output when it's wrong. I think the way to stay in charge is to start now: use it every day, check what it produces and keep the final call. That's also why I took the Claude Academy courses this year.

FLEX-DATAProduct OwnerLOTTE C&F Vietnam · 2025–now

Finding where BNPL applicants drop out

I built the GA4 tracking that shows where new LOTTE FLEX users drop out, and which drop-offs our team can fix.

Role
Product Owner duties, UI/UX
Worked with
Mobile developers, BA, Business, Risk, IT
Scope
Onboarding flow
Data
GA4 funnels, checked against internal data
Tools
GA4, Google Sheets, Jira, Confluence

The question

Every new user goes through several required steps before they get a credit limit, and two of the approvals belong to a financial partner. The team needed to know where applicants drop out, and which of those drop-offs our own screens cause.

What I did

  1. Wrote the tracking plan: screen and action events, funnel milestones and a parameter dictionary, under one naming convention.
  2. Worked with mobile developers on the implementation and checked each event against live GA4 data.
  3. Built a master funnel with ten milestones and three detailed funnels, one per stage.
  4. Compared GA4 with internal data before trusting any pattern.
  5. Wrote the monthly report: what each funnel shows and what Business, Risk and IT should do next.

Decisions I made along the way

D-01

Patterns from GA4, totals from internal data

Found

GA4 didn't capture every registration yet: tracking was still settling, and older app versions don't send events.

Decided

Use GA4 to read trends and analyze user behavior, not as an official source that replaces internal records. Official numbers come from internal records, and the report says so clearly.

Trade-off

No exact counts from GA4 for now, but priorities rest on patterns that hold. Coverage improves as people update the app.

D-02

Sort drop-offs by who can fix them

Found

Most drop-offs happened at steps the financial partner controls; only a small share were on our own screens.

Decided

Tag every funnel step with its owner. Partner steps go to a joint review with the partner and internal teams; our own screens get UX fixes.

Trade-off

The biggest losses aren't ours to fix directly, so progress there depends on the partner.

D-03

Leaving eKYC is not the same as failing it

Found

More users left eKYC midway than failed it. The verification step is run by the partner, so we can't see exactly where people stop.

Decided

Prepare users earlier: tell them which documents they need before they start, and ask the partner for step-level data.

Trade-off

Some unprepared users stop earlier, but they can come back ready, and we spend less on users who won't finish.

D-04

One screen, most of the loss

Found

One personal-details screen lost far more users than any other input screen, and caused most of the drop-off in that stage.

Decided

Proposed splitting it into two shorter screens and letting people pick their address instead of typing it.

Trade-off

One more screen in the flow, in exchange for shorter, clearer input on each.

Chart types that were used

Outcome

The team now has one shared view of onboarding, with every drop-off sorted by who can fix it. For the financial partner's approval steps, the report lists what to check with the partner, Risk and IT. On our side no big redesign was needed: the work narrowed to a specific step in the flow, and the proposal is in the backlog.

Ran delivery for two teams
